Disaster Resilience for Rural NSW Communities

Rural communities in NSW often lack localised environmental data and experience poor data coverage, problems that are severely compounded during hazardous weather events. This limits the ability of emergency services and communities to access the information they need for disaster preparedness and response.

This project extends the previous ANU capstone project, a community disaster resilience initiative (a flood monitoring system built on a Meshtastic network), by investigating an alternative LoRa-based protocol MeshCore, and broadening monitoring to cover multiple hazard types including bushfires, landslides, severe storms, and drought. The final expected output is a prototype hazard detection sensor.

The project is part of the Disaster Ready Fund (DRF) initiative, funded by the National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA) and running until March 2028. All work produced is designed to be handed forward to future teams.

Phase 1: Design Decision Report Workstreams

The first phase of the project runs three parallel workstreams, each tackling a distinct technical challenge. The second phase integrates their outputs into a unified, deployable prototype.

Workstream 1

Communication Network

Investigation and small-scale testing of MeshCore as the primary communication network for emergency data transfer in rural areas.

  • Small-scale MeshCore network testing
  • Communication range & reliability evaluation
  • Software considerations for embedded development (Rust vs. C)
  • Exploring the MQTT protocol for data transmission
  • Ensuring ACMA compliance
📄 View Report
Workstream 2

Sensor and Hazard Assessment

Feasibility study and options assessment of consumer-off-the-shelf (COTS) components to be used in the multi-hazard sensor nodes. Focuses on selecting appropriate sensors for the range of hazards prevalent in rural NSW.

  • Research of environmental hazards (flooding, bushfire, landslide, storm, drought)
  • COTS sensor suitability assessment (rainfall, relative humidity, temperature, wind)
  • Component selection and options assessment report
📄 View Report
Workstream 3

Power & Enclosure

Preliminary research of the physical harness for securing the sensor design to various landscapes, alongside power supply and regulation design to ensure low power consumption and long-lasting battery life in the field.

  • Battery, solar panel, and enclosure recommendations
  • Solar charging and battery life optimisation
  • Environmental durability and weatherproofing
  • Physical enclosure and mounting harness design
📄 View Report

Phase 2: Prototyping and Testing

Combining the outputs of Phase 1's three workstreams into a unified, deployable hazard detection prototype.

Workstream 1

Firmware

Development of embedded software to interface with sensors, manage power states, and handle communication over the MeshCore network on the RAK platform.

  • Sensor driver integration and data handling
  • Power management and low-power sleep states
  • MeshCore network integration and payload handling
  • Local bench testing of firmware builds
📁 View Team Folder
Workstream 2

Prototyping

Physical assembly and integration of sensor, power, and enclosure subsystems into a working hazard detection node, ready for field deployment.

  • Hardware assembly and subsystem integration
  • Enclosure and mounting harness assembly
  • Local and field testing of the assembled prototype
  • Iteration based on Test and Evaluation Plan results

Project Schedule & Milestones

The project runs throughout 2026, structured around four audit milestones with a spiral systems engineering approach.

Completed Semester 1 — Week 4

1 — Orientation

Established team communications, reviewed previous project repository and handover documentation, made contact with project host, and produced initial planning documents.

Completed Semester 1 — Week 11

2 — Investigation

Research into communication platforms (Meshtastic, MeshCore, and alternatives) and hazard detection feasibility. All devices for detection to be decided by this milestone.

In Progress Semester 2 — Week 4

3 — Integration

First prototypes of hazard detection device(s) complete and ready for field testing within the ACT region. Field testing plans established and underway.

Upcoming Semester 2 — Week 11

4 — Evaluation

All field testing complete, findings report drafted. Team reflections documented for future project onboarding and handover.
